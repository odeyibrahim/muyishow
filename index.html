Legacy & values

A standard, not a nostalgia

Our principal trained at Fani-Kayode & Sowemimo, established in 1970 and among Nigeria’s oldest indigenous law firms. He rose to Senior Counsel at its successor practice before founding this firm.

That training gave him a discipline: rigorous preparation, institutional memory and a plain understanding of how law sits alongside government, commerce and community. We apply it daily.

A name with a history opens doors, but it is not why clients stay. The standing of this firm rests on the work behind the name: more than two decades of practice across corporate, banking, insurance, energy, property and litigation, an education at Cardiff and Essex, and a network that crosses jurisdictions.

We are a boutique by design: focused practice areas, deep Of Counsel alliances and direct access to the principal. We would rather build specialist depth than scale for its own sake.

Why Abuja

After years in Lagos, the firm moved its principal office to the Federal Capital Territory. Government permits, mining and energy licensing, federal constitutional questions and public-sector compliance all converge there. Being in Abuja means being at the table, not travelling to it.

The same position lets us act as a dependable Nigerian anchor for inbound capital, joint ventures and cross-border transactions, working alongside foreign counsel and advisers rather than apart from them.

Our Abeokuta office serves growing businesses and entrepreneurs in Ogun State, where proximity to Lagos, a fast-moving property market and significant mineral resources are producing increasingly sophisticated commercial activity.

What we value

  • Commercial pragmatismWe assess what the law means for your bottom line, not only what it says.
  • Contracts that create valueA well-negotiated agreement is a business asset. We draft to protect position and allocate risk sensibly.
  • Counsel who think commerciallyWe help clients move decisively, weighing risk against return.
  • Institutional memory, appliedWhat we learned inside a firm that advised heads of state informs how we handle regulatory and political complexity.

  • Client management as disciplineRetainers are run with the rigour of a single transaction. Filings, licences and compliance calendars are tracked ahead of time.
  • Depth over breadthFocused practices backed by Of Counsel, rather than all things to all clients.

The Principal

Muyiwa Sowemimo

LL.B. (Cardiff), P.G.C.E., M.A. (Essex)

Called to the Nigerian Bar in 2004, Muyiwa has practised for over two decades and rose to Senior Counsel before founding his own boutique firm.

He began his career at the Petroleum Trust Fund in Abuja, vetting and monitoring government infrastructure and community projects. Since then he has handled matters across corporate, banking, insurance, energy, property and litigation. That range shapes how he manages every client.

His own practice centres on high-stakes civil and commercial litigation, high-value liability and professional negligence claims, trusts and estates, government permits and regulatory compliance, corporate and commercial work, entertainment law frameworks, and the stewardship of long-term retainers. Where a matter calls for a specialist, he knows when to lead and when to bring in the right Of Counsel.

Full profile

Michael Figbele is a Nigerian legal practitioner with over a decade of broad courtroom and advisory experience, and Of Counsel for Abeokuta base and the wider Ogun–Lagos corridor. Called to the Nigerian Bar in 2014, Michael has built a litigation practice grounded in consistent courtroom engagement — civil and criminal proceedings, fundamental rights enforcement, and appellate advocacy before the superior courts of record.

His property practice is particularly relevant to the corridor in which the firm’s Abeokuta office sits: title investigation and verification, due diligence and property risk assessment, perfection of documentation, land recovery and trespass litigation, and the resolution of family and community land disputes. For a region experiencing rapid property and commercial development, that working knowledge is applied rather than theoretical.

He also brings substantial experience in employment and industrial relations, including proceedings before the National Industrial Court of Nigeria — wrongful termination, reinstatement claims, and workplace disputes involving both corporate employers and public institutions. His litigation record includes successful reinstatement matters for dismissed public officers and transport personnel, alongside complex land, commercial, and contractual disputes.

His practice extends to corporate and commercial advisory, contract drafting and negotiation, debt recovery, and alternative dispute resolution — mediation, negotiation, and arbitration-related support — with a geographical reach spanning Ogun, Lagos, Edo, and the FCT.

At Muyi Sowemimo & Co., Michael is engaged where a matter in the Abeokuta corridor calls for local litigation depth, property due diligence, or employment-related proceedings — an available and responsive resource on the ground, extending the firm’s coverage across the Ogun–Lagos axis without diluting the boutique model.

Privacy notice

Last updated 17 November 2026

Who we are

Muyi Sowemimo & Co. (“the firm”, “we”) is the data controller for personal data collected through this website. Contact: info@muyi-sowemimolegal.com.

What we collect

Only what you give us through the general enquiry and international referral forms: name, firm, email, phone, jurisdiction and the details of your message. We also use basic technical data, such as browser type and pages visited, to keep the site secure and working.

Why we use it

To respond to your enquiry or referral, to assess whether we can act, and to meet our legal and professional obligations, including conflict checks. We rely on your consent, our legitimate interest in answering enquiries, and legal obligation, as the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 allows. We do not use your data for marketing unless you ask us to.

Confidentiality

Please do not send privileged or sensitive material through the forms before we have confirmed an engagement. Messages sent through the website do not create a lawyer-client relationship.

Sharing and storage

Form submissions are routed to the firm’s email. We share data only with service providers who host or secure this site, with our Of Counsel where a matter needs them, or where the law requires. We keep enquiry data only as long as needed for these purposes and our professional record-keeping duties.

Your rights

You may ask to access, correct, delete or restrict the use of your data, object to its use, or withdraw consent, by emailing us. You may also complain to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission.

Cookies

This site uses no advertising or tracking cookies. If analytics are added later, this notice will be updated.
